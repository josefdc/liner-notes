# Audio accuracy, 2026-10-25

Tempo and key accuracy of the audio service (`services/audio`) on annotated datasets, its agreement with sourced tempo and key values on the golden set's Deezer previews, its style fingerprint against Discogs styles, and what each tempo estimator costs, measured by `evals/audio` (`uv run python -m evals_audio`). The harness imports the service and calls its own `analyze_signals`, so the numbers are what the service returns, gate and withholding rules included. Rendered from `audio-2026-10-25.json`, which holds the per-clip results: track ids, estimates and scores. It stores no reference labels and no audio file names, but an estimate with its score often gives the reference away (a MIREX score of 1, 0.5, 0.3 or 0.2 fixes the key; an Acc1 hit pins the tempo within 4 %), so those results are derived from the annotations: the licence and credits under each dataset apply to them.

Runs in this report: tempo on Ballroom (ISMIR 2004 tempo induction contest) (librosa, essentia, beat-this); tempo on GiantSteps Tempo (librosa, essentia, beat-this); key on GiantSteps Key, as GiantSteps+ (Faraldo 2017 revision) (librosa, essentia); the golden set's Deezer previews (librosa, essentia, beat-this); costs on GiantSteps Tempo clips (librosa, essentia, beat-this); what the tempo rules compare on Ballroom (ISMIR 2004 tempo induction contest) (librosa, essentia, beat-this); what the key rules compare on GiantSteps Key, as GiantSteps+ (Faraldo 2017 revision) (librosa, essentia); what the tempo rules compare on GiantSteps Tempo (librosa, essentia, beat-this); the generated controls (librosa, essentia, beat-this). beat-this is measured here only: the service does not ship it, and the service's default estimator is not changed by this report.

## How to read it

- **n** is every selected clip with verified audio and a usable reference. **Answered** clips got a value from the service; **withheld** clips got none; **failed** clips raised an error (none is expected). Coverage is answered / n.
- **correct / n** counts a withheld clip as no correct value. It answers *how often does a user get a right value*. **correct / answered** is the share of returned values that are right. It answers *how often is a value the service shows right*, and it depends on which clips the service chose to answer: two estimators that answer different clips are compared in the paired table, not by this column.
- **Acc1**: the tempo is within 4 % of the reference. **Acc2**: within 4 % of the reference times 1, 2, 3, 1/2 or 1/3 (octave and meter errors forgiven).
- **MIREX** key score: 1 same key, 0.5 a perfect fifth above in the same mode, 0.3 relative, 0.2 parallel, 0 otherwise; shown as the summed score over the denominator. **exact**: the same key only.
- **Intervals** are 95 %: Wilson score intervals for Acc1, Acc2 and exact; a BCa bootstrap (10,000 resamples, fixed seed) for MIREX, with no interval when every score is equal. Each interval rests on the denominator printed next to it, which for correct / answered can be far below n.
- **Paired MIREX difference**: most paired key differences are zero, and a bootstrap interval can then cover far less than 95 %. The interval here is a paired t interval whose spread includes two pseudo-differences at -1 and +1, the ends of the range, so it never collapses to zero width. Each row says how often it covered the mean when that row's own differences were resampled. It is wide at small n and is not conservative in general: on dense differences it can cover less than 95 % (`evals/audio/scripts/paired_coverage.py` simulates both). The sign-flip p-value tests "no difference" over every sign pattern of the nonzero differences (exact up to 16 of them).
- **p-values** are exact where it is cheap (McNemar; sign-flip; the AUC's permutation test up to 200,000 orderings) and Monte Carlo at a fixed seed otherwise, as labelled, computed as (hits + 1) / (resamples + 1). Each is for one comparison. This report gives 110 distinct ones (the default-estimator table repeats some of them); about 5.5 of them are expected under 0.05 by chance alone, so none should be read as a finding without a correction for the number of tests.
- **beat-this** (checkpoint `final0`, CPU, no DBN) is not part of the service; the harness runs it behind the service's estimator interface, so its tempo goes through the service's own rules: the onset-periodicity minimum, 40-208 BPM, the tempo refined from the beats on onsets, and the steadiness check. It gives no confidence (so no curve and no confidence minimum) and no key. Its authors trained `final0` on every dataset they used except GTZAN, Ballroom included: an estimator marked *trained on this dataset* is shown on its own training data: those cells are not evidence about new music and its pair tables there are not printed.
- **Agreement** (golden set) is not accuracy. Deezer's `bpm` is Deezer's own estimate, a sourced hint; the set's tempo and key labels are nominal sheet-music markings. A disagreement can be either side's error. The tolerances are Acc1/Acc2's (within 4 %; up to octave and meter) and MIREX/exact's.
- **hit@k**: the fingerprint's k strongest styles (`Genre---Style`) include one whose genre and style are both on the Discogs master (or release) of the earliest single, EP or album the recording is on. The constant baseline gives every track the same k styles, the model's average output (the highest summed score over the top-10 lists of the scored previews), so it uses no label; it is not the strongest constant. The best constant found with the labels (greedy cover) is printed as an optimistic reference. The style model was trained on Discogs styles (Discogs-4M, about 3.3 million tracks), possibly of these very releases: a hit can be recall rather than judgement.
- **Costs** are measured on one machine, each estimator in its own fresh process with one thread (forced); times vary from run to run. The image cost is what an estimator adds to the service's image as it ships (which already holds Essentia, for the fingerprint): installed bytes, as an image layer holds them uncompressed, and wheel bytes, plus model files; no image was built. Peak memory includes one-time compilation (numba for librosa); the memory after the first analysed clip is shown next to it.
- **Lifted rules**: the service withholds a tempo under its rules (the periodicity and confidence minimums, the steadiness check). The estimate it computed is kept apart and scored, so a comparison can be made with a rule lifted. That is calibration, never what a user gets. The steadiness check compares each third of the window with librosa's own tempo estimate on that third.
- **Comparability**: one 30 s window per clip and the excluded labels make these numbers incomparable with published figures for the same datasets (computed on full excerpts, usually on every labelled track).

## Calibration of the withholding rules

The service withholds a tempo or a key when its rules say the evidence is too weak (the steadiness check, the confidence and periodicity minimums, the key evidence rule and the key strength minimum). This section sets those rules from measured results: on the annotated clips of the calibration runs below and on generated controls whose truth is known by construction. Each selected clip is a **calibration** clip or an **evaluation** clip, fixed by a SHA-256 of the dataset and its track id (about half each). Values are chosen on the calibration clips and the controls; the evaluation clips judge each change once more. The choice is made again here from the recorded clips (`evals_audio.calibration.select`), so it can be checked against the values the service has.

- The procedure was written down before the calibration runs. A **loosening** (answers the old value withheld) is accepted when its extra answers are right (Acc1 for tempo, exact for key) with a Wilson 95 % lower bound of at least 50 % (L1), at most 10 points less often than the old answers (L2), wrong even forgiving octave and meter (key: unrelated) at most 10 points more often (L3), and no control goes from right to wrong (L4); the loosest value that passes is taken. A **tightening** is accepted when it removes a value a control shows wrongly (T1), lowers correct / n by at most 2 points on each dataset and withholds answers that are right less often than those it keeps (T2); the least strict value that passes is taken. A rule the service keeps per estimator (the confidence and strength minimums) is decided on that estimator's clips; a shared rule on Essentia's (the default) and checked on librosa's: a change librosa's clips reject is not made. beat-this is shown under the service's rules and decides nothing.
- Only changes that withhold more are shipped. A loosening the procedure picks is shown but not shipped: it rests on these clips alone, and two loosenings chosen this way in earlier versions of this calibration gave keys to material with no key.
- Each change is then judged on the evaluation clips: a loosening that fails L1-L3 or a tightening that fails T2 there is reverted to the value before it. The controls are the ones the choice saw, so a change made for a control is not tested on new controls there; a change that alters no evaluation clip stays.
- The procedure is applied as written before the calibration runs. After a first sweep of the calibration clips, revisions were proposed and used in a first version of this calibration: judging the key strength before the key evidence rules, a family that drops the spectral-line rules over a beat for Essentia, choosing the tightening that fixes most controls, and requiring a tightening to break no control. They were made after calibration results had been seen; the line-rule family decided that version's main result and let Essentia key a pitchless drum track (the service's own drum kit at 128 BPM, kit seed 3). None of them is used here.
- A second version read the plan's "librosa checked" as librosa shown only, where the first had read it as a veto; the change was made after the numbers had been seen. It let through a pitch-class level of 0.1, which gave keys to one pitched source (a one-note bass over drums, a church bell, a tabla), and a tolerance of 0.04 that librosa's clips reject. The veto is restored here, and no loosening is shipped.
- The evaluation clips were scored in each version: the first time from measurements rounded to six decimals, which made a 0.04 tolerance look as if it fixed a librosa control (one third was 0.96 of the tempo to the last bit). The values here follow from the written procedure on the full-precision recording, applying only changes that withhold more; none was chosen or changed in response to an evaluation score.
- Before the plan was written, earlier reports had shown why 39 of 50 GiantSteps+ keys were withheld, some of them on clips that are now evaluation clips; that is what drew attention to the key evidence rules.
- The pitchless controls drawn at 30 seeds each (two drum kits, noise with a beat, a siren over drums, clicks) were added after the drum track above was found, and the one-pitch-class ones (a one-note bass, octave riffs, bells alone and over drums, tabla, toms, a cowbell, a held synth note) after the level of 0.1 was. No value had been set on them; the choice sees them like the other controls.
- The families are taken in turn, each with the earlier choices in place: steadiness check: librosa's tempo on each third's onsets; steadiness check: tolerance; steadiness check: beat drift within a third (window, tolerance); tempo confidence minimum; onset periodicity minimum (tempo); key evidence: share of energy in spectral lines (no sustained pitch), of the mix or of its harmonic part; key evidence: pitch classes (fewer than three), by vector and level; key strength minimum.
- Before means the service's values at commit `ec7d24b`; after means the values the runs were made with (commit `78555ce`).

Measured on 20 clips (20 giantsteps-tempo tracks drawn with numpy default_rng(1), 30 s windows at clip seed 1). Each clip's tempo is computed on freshly decoded signals, so a call includes what the estimator needs (the onset envelope, its model). A clip the onset-periodicity minimum stops never reaches the estimator's own work (as in the service); those clips are counted and not timed. The first clip that does reach it pays the one-time costs (numba compilation for librosa, the first pass through a model) and is shown apart, with its id and the memory right after it; the peak includes that one-time memory. The service's own process also holds the fingerprint models and computes the gate, loudness and sections, which are not counted here. RSS is the resident memory of the process; the service figure includes Essentia's libraries when they are installed.

The service image installs the `models` extra (essentia-tensorflow, for the fingerprint) whatever the estimator, so Essentia adds nothing to it; an estimator's image cost is the packages its extra needs that the service's lock does not have.
