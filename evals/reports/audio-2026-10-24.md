# Audio accuracy, 2026-10-24

Tempo and key accuracy of the audio service (`services/audio`) on annotated datasets, its agreement with sourced tempo and key values on the golden set's Deezer previews, its style fingerprint against Discogs styles, and what each tempo estimator costs, measured by `evals/audio` (`uv run python -m evals_audio`). The harness imports the service and calls its own `analyze_signals`, so the numbers are what the service returns, gate and withholding rules included. Rendered from `audio-2026-10-24.json`, which holds the per-clip results: track ids, estimates and scores. It stores no reference labels and no audio file names, but an estimate with its score often gives the reference away (a MIREX score of 1, 0.5, 0.3 or 0.2 fixes the key; an Acc1 hit pins the tempo within 4 %), so those results are derived from the annotations: the licence and credits under each dataset apply to them.

Runs in this report: tempo on Ballroom (ISMIR 2004 tempo induction contest) (librosa, essentia, beat-this); tempo on GiantSteps Tempo (librosa, essentia, beat-this); key on GiantSteps Key, as GiantSteps+ (Faraldo 2017 revision) (librosa, essentia); the golden set's Deezer previews (librosa, essentia, beat-this); costs on GiantSteps Tempo clips (librosa, essentia, beat-this). beat-this is measured here only: the service does not ship it, and the service's default estimator is not changed by this report.

## How to read it

- **n** is every selected clip with verified audio and a usable reference. **Answered** clips got a value from the service; **withheld** clips got none; **failed** clips raised an error (none is expected). Coverage is answered / n.
- **correct / n** counts a withheld clip as no correct value. It answers *how often does a user get a right value*. **correct / answered** is the share of returned values that are right. It answers *how often is a value the service shows right*, and it depends on which clips the service chose to answer: two estimators that answer different clips are compared in the paired table, not by this column.
- **Acc1**: the tempo is within 4 % of the reference. **Acc2**: within 4 % of the reference times 1, 2, 3, 1/2 or 1/3 (octave and meter errors forgiven).
- **MIREX** key score: 1 same key, 0.5 a perfect fifth above in the same mode, 0.3 relative, 0.2 parallel, 0 otherwise; shown as the summed score over the denominator. **exact**: the same key only.
- **Intervals** are 95 %: Wilson score intervals for Acc1, Acc2 and exact; a BCa bootstrap (10,000 resamples, fixed seed) for MIREX, with no interval when every score is equal. Each interval rests on the denominator printed next to it, which for correct / answered can be far below n.
- **Paired MIREX difference**: most paired key differences are zero, and a bootstrap interval can then cover far less than 95 %. The interval here is a paired t interval whose spread includes two pseudo-differences at -1 and +1, the ends of the range, so it never collapses to zero width. Each row says how often it covered the mean when that row's own differences were resampled. It is wide at small n and is not conservative in general: on dense differences it can cover less than 95 % (`evals/audio/scripts/paired_coverage.py` simulates both). The sign-flip p-value tests "no difference" over every sign pattern of the nonzero differences (exact up to 16 of them).
- **p-values** are exact where it is cheap (McNemar; sign-flip; the AUC's permutation test up to 200,000 orderings) and Monte Carlo at a fixed seed otherwise, as labelled, computed as (hits + 1) / (resamples + 1). Each is for one comparison. This report prints 81; about 4.0 of them are expected under 0.05 by chance alone, so none should be read as a finding without a correction for the number of tests.
- **beat-this** (checkpoint `final0`, CPU, no DBN) is not part of the service; the harness runs it behind the service's estimator interface, so its tempo goes through the service's own rules: the onset-periodicity minimum, 40-208 BPM, the tempo refined from the beats on onsets, and the steadiness check. It gives no confidence (so no curve and no confidence minimum) and no key. Its authors trained `final0` on every dataset they used except GTZAN, Ballroom included: an estimator marked *trained on this dataset* is shown on its own training data, and those cells and pairs are not evidence about new music.
- **Agreement** (golden set) is not accuracy. Deezer's `bpm` is Deezer's own estimate, a sourced hint; the set's tempo and key labels are nominal sheet-music markings. A disagreement can be either side's error. The tolerances are Acc1/Acc2's (within 4 %; up to octave and meter) and MIREX/exact's.
- **hit@k**: the fingerprint's k strongest styles (`Genre---Style`) include one whose genre and style are both on the Discogs master (or release) of the earliest single, EP or album the recording is on. The constant baseline gives every track the same k styles, the ones with the highest summed score over the run's fingerprints, so it uses no label. The style model was trained on Discogs styles (Discogs-4M, about 3.3 million tracks), possibly of these very releases: a hit can be recall rather than judgement.
- **Costs** are measured on one machine, each estimator in its own fresh process with one thread; times vary from run to run. Install sizes are what an extra adds to the harness's base install (installed bytes, as an image layer would hold them uncompressed, and wheel bytes); no image was built.
- **Comparability**: one 30 s window per clip and the excluded labels make these numbers incomparable with published figures for the same datasets (computed on full excerpts, usually on every labelled track).
