# Audio accuracy, 2026-10-24

Tempo and key accuracy of the audio service (`services/audio`) on annotated datasets, its agreement with sourced tempo and key values on the golden set's Deezer previews, its style fingerprint against Discogs styles, and what each tempo estimator costs, measured by `evals/audio` (`uv run python -m evals_audio`). The harness imports the service and calls its own `analyze_signals`, so the numbers are what the service returns, gate and withholding rules included. Rendered from `audio-2026-10-24.json`, which holds the per-clip results: track ids, estimates and scores. It stores no reference labels and no audio file names, but an estimate with its score often gives the reference away (a MIREX score of 1, 0.5, 0.3 or 0.2 fixes the key; an Acc1 hit pins the tempo within 4 %), so those results are derived from the annotations: the licence and credits under each dataset apply to them.

Runs in this report: tempo on Ballroom (ISMIR 2004 tempo induction contest) (librosa, essentia, beat-this); tempo on GiantSteps Tempo (librosa, essentia, beat-this); key on GiantSteps Key, as GiantSteps+ (Faraldo 2017 revision) (librosa, essentia); the golden set's Deezer previews (librosa, essentia, beat-this); costs on GiantSteps Tempo clips (librosa, essentia, beat-this). beat-this is measured here only: the service does not ship it, and the service's default estimator is not changed by this report.

## How to read it

- **n** is every selected clip with verified audio and a usable reference. **Answered** clips got a value from the service; **withheld** clips got none; **failed** clips raised an error (none is expected). Coverage is answered / n.
- **correct / n** counts a withheld clip as no correct value. It answers *how often does a user get a right value*. **correct / answered** is the share of returned values that are right. It answers *how often is a value the service shows right*, and it depends on which clips the service chose to answer: two estimators that answer different clips are compared in the paired table, not by this column.
- **Acc1**: the tempo is within 4 % of the reference. **Acc2**: within 4 % of the reference times 1, 2, 3, 1/2 or 1/3 (octave and meter errors forgiven).
- **MIREX** key score: 1 same key, 0.5 a perfect fifth above in the same mode, 0.3 relative, 0.2 parallel, 0 otherwise; shown as the summed score over the denominator. **exact**: the same key only.
- **Intervals** are 95 %: Wilson score intervals for Acc1, Acc2 and exact; a BCa bootstrap (10,000 resamples, fixed seed) for MIREX, with no interval when every score is equal. Each interval rests on the denominator printed next to it, which for correct / answered can be far below n.
- **Paired MIREX difference**: most paired key differences are zero, and a bootstrap interval can then cover far less than 95 %. The interval here is a paired t interval whose spread includes two pseudo-differences at -1 and +1, the ends of the range, so it never collapses to zero width. Each row says how often it covered the mean when that row's own differences were resampled. It is wide at small n and is not conservative in general: on dense differences it can cover less than 95 % (`evals/audio/scripts/paired_coverage.py` simulates both). The sign-flip p-value tests "no difference" over every sign pattern of the nonzero differences (exact up to 16 of them).
- **p-values** are exact where it is cheap (McNemar; sign-flip; the AUC's permutation test up to 200,000 orderings) and Monte Carlo at a fixed seed otherwise, as labelled, computed as (hits + 1) / (resamples + 1). Each is for one comparison. This report gives 79 distinct ones (the default-estimator table repeats some of them); about 4.0 of them are expected under 0.05 by chance alone, so none should be read as a finding without a correction for the number of tests.
- **beat-this** (checkpoint `final0`, CPU, no DBN) is not part of the service; the harness runs it behind the service's estimator interface, so its tempo goes through the service's own rules: the onset-periodicity minimum, 40-208 BPM, the tempo refined from the beats on onsets, and the steadiness check. It gives no confidence (so no curve and no confidence minimum) and no key. Its authors trained `final0` on every dataset they used except GTZAN, Ballroom included: an estimator marked *trained on this dataset* is shown on its own training data: those cells are not evidence about new music and its pair tables there are not printed.
- **Agreement** (golden set) is not accuracy. Deezer's `bpm` is Deezer's own estimate, a sourced hint; the set's tempo and key labels are nominal sheet-music markings. A disagreement can be either side's error. The tolerances are Acc1/Acc2's (within 4 %; up to octave and meter) and MIREX/exact's.
- **hit@k**: the fingerprint's k strongest styles (`Genre---Style`) include one whose genre and style are both on the Discogs master (or release) of the earliest single, EP or album the recording is on. The constant baseline gives every track the same k styles, the model's average output (the highest summed score over the top-10 lists of the scored previews), so it uses no label; it is not the strongest constant. The best constant found with the labels (greedy cover) is printed as an optimistic reference. The style model was trained on Discogs styles (Discogs-4M, about 3.3 million tracks), possibly of these very releases: a hit can be recall rather than judgement.
- **Costs** are measured on one machine, each estimator in its own fresh process with one thread (forced); times vary from run to run. The image cost is what an estimator adds to the service's image as it ships (which already holds Essentia, for the fingerprint): installed bytes, as an image layer holds them uncompressed, and wheel bytes, plus model files; no image was built. Peak memory includes one-time compilation (numba for librosa); the memory after the first analysed clip is shown next to it.
- **Lifted rules**: the service withholds a tempo under its rules (the periodicity and confidence minimums, the steadiness check). The estimate it computed is kept apart and scored, so a comparison can be made with a rule lifted. That is calibration, never what a user gets. The steadiness check compares each third of the window with librosa's own tempo estimate on that third.
- **Comparability**: one 30 s window per clip and the excluded labels make these numbers incomparable with published figures for the same datasets (computed on full excerpts, usually on every labelled track).

## Default estimator

The service's default tempo and key estimator is `essentia` (what `estimators.select_estimator()` picks when `AUDIO_ESTIMATOR` is not set, with Essentia installed as in the service's image).

Criterion: an estimator replaces the default when, on data it was not trained on, it gives more correct tempos (Acc1: within 4 %) over n than the default as the service serves them (withholding rules included), with an exact McNemar p under 0.05 on the same clips, and its cost to the image, the memory and the analysis time is worth that gain. The cost is weighed, not thresholded.

Evidence in this report, each candidate against `essentia` on the same clips (correct / n of each, exact McNemar p), on data the candidate was not trained on:

- Cost of `librosa` against `essentia`: 0.05 s against 0.307 s of CPU per analysed clip (0.16 times as much); peak RSS 644.4 MB against 581.8 MB; it adds nothing (already in the image) to the service image, where `essentia` adds nothing (already in the image).
- Cost of `beat-this` against `essentia`: 3.86 s against 0.307 s of CPU per analysed clip (13 times as much); peak RSS 879.9 MB against 581.8 MB; it adds 745.0 MB installed + 81.1 MB model to the service image, where `essentia` adds nothing (already in the image).

Decision: the service keeps `essentia` under the current withholding rules. As served, no candidate gives significantly more correct tempos on data it was not trained on, and the cost weighs against a change (beat-this also has no key: the service would need it for tempo and another estimator for key). With the withholding rules lifted, `beat-this` is ahead on giantsteps-tempo (150 vs 98 of 200, p < 0.001), so the default is to be revisited once those rules, the steadiness check first, are recalibrated.

```sh
cd evals/audio && uv run python -m evals_audio --suite tempo --dataset giantsteps-tempo --limit 200 --seed 1
```

- Run 2026-10-24T17:30:55Z on commit `373df9b`; 422 s wall with 4 worker processes.
- Analyzer: `ANALYZER_VERSION` 2.0.0 `2.0.0+librosa`, `2.0.0+essentia`, `2.0.0+beat-this` (the fingerprint models were not loaded; tempo and key do not use them).
- Environment: python 3.12.3, librosa 1.0.0, numpy 2.3.5, scipy 1.16.3, mirdata 1.0.0, essentia-tensorflow 2.1b6.dev1389, beat-this 1.1.0, torch 2.11.0+cpu.
- Seeds: selection 1, clip windows 1, bootstrap 1. Selection: 200 of 661 usable tracks drawn with numpy default_rng(1). Clips: 30 s, the start fixed by the clip seed and the track id (SHA-256); a recording of 30 s or less is used whole. The 200 recordings measured are 80.2-120.0 s long (used whole: 0).

- 664 two-minute Beatport previews of electronic dance music.
- mirdata does not download this audio; the harness uses the locations in the dataset's download script and checks each file's MD5.
- Published GiantSteps Tempo figures are computed on the whole two-minute previews, mostly against the v1 labels; here one 30 s window per preview is scored against v2, and the window can fall on an intro, a breakdown or the fade. Not comparable.
- The v2 reference is the crowd tempo with the highest share. 184 of the 200 clips have more than one crowd tempo and 24 have a top two within 0.2 of each other, so the reference is fragile there. The v1 reference (Knees et al. 2015) differs by more than 4 % on 37 of 200 clips; against v1, Acc1 correct / n would be `librosa` 34/200, `essentia` 34/200, `beat-this` 38/200.

### Accuracy vs coverage

Answered clips ranked by the service's confidence, highest first; each row keeps the top k (coverage = k / n) and gives correct / k with its interval. Rows with fewer than 10 clips are left out, except the last row (every answered clip). Neighbouring rows share most of their clips and their intervals overlap, so a rise or fall between rows is not evidence on its own. Whether the confidence ranks answers at all is the AUC line under each table: the chance that a correct answer has a higher confidence than a wrong one (0.5 is random order), with a one-sided Mann-Whitney p-value against random order.

A clip the onset-periodicity minimum stops never reaches the estimator's own work (as in the service); those clips are counted and not timed. The first clip that does reach it pays the one-time costs (numba compilation for librosa, the first pass through a model) and is shown apart, with its id and the memory right after it; the peak includes that one-time memory. The service's own process also holds the fingerprint models and computes the gate, loudness and sections, which are not counted here. RSS is the resident memory of the process; the service figure includes Essentia's libraries when they are installed.

The service image installs the `models` extra (essentia-tensorflow, for the fingerprint) whatever the estimator, so Essentia adds nothing to it; an estimator's image cost is the packages its extra needs that the service's lock does not have.

## Reproducing

Datasets are fetched into `~/liner-notes-data` (or `LINER_NOTES_DATA`), outside the repository, under a cap (`LINER_NOTES_DATA_CAP_BYTES`, 2 GB by default). Only the selected tracks' audio is written, each checked against mirdata's MD5. See `evals/audio/README.md`.
